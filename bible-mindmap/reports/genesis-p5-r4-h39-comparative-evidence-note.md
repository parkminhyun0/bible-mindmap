## Target

## Public comparative evidence collected

1. Brown-Driver-Briggs presentation (BibleHub BDB H39): identifies the form as a masculine proper name, glosses the name analysis as “a father is El,” explicitly labels it a South-Arabic name, and compares a Sabaean personal-name formation `אבמעתֿתר` with the explanatory pattern “a father is Athtar.” The entry cites Halévy and D. H. Müller / ZDMG 1883 as the comparative basis.
2. Strong presentation (BibleHub Strong H39): derives the entry from `אב` plus an otherwise unused/probably foreign element and gives the explanatory wording “father of Mael (apparently some Arab tribe).”
3. International Standard Bible Encyclopedia public-domain entry: gives “my father is God” or “God is father,” and states that the name is linguistically South Arabian, pointing to early South-Arabian/Arabic epigraphic literature.
